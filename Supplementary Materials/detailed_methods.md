`Expanded Technical Summary`

**Sample.** PGBI-elevated (mania-risk) subsample of LAMS (Longitudinal Assessment of Manic Symptoms). Youth were recruited from outpatient mental-health clinics in Ohio and western Pennsylvania, ages 6 to 12 at baseline, and preferentially enrolled for elevated manic symptoms, operationally defined as a Parent General Behavior Inventory 10-item Mania score (PGBI-10M) of 12 or higher. Diagnoses were assigned by consensus from semi-structured K-SADS (Kiddie Schedule for Affective Disorders and Schizophrenia) interviews of youth and caregiver. SCID-II BPD items were assessed at follow-up assessment waves each year when the participant was of age (13+); concurrent validators were drawn at each youth's most recent completed assessment.

**Indicators.** 8 binary SCID-II (Structured Clinical Interview for DSM-IV Axis II Disorders, Borderline Personality Disorder identity-disturbance module) items, recoded 0/1 to 1/2 for `poLCA`'s integer requirement. Latent class analysis was fit in the R package `poLCA` (nrep = 200, maxiter = 5000, tol = 1e-8; local-maxima guard, solutions confirmed to reproduce across starts). All validators (irritability, aggression, impulsivity, functioning, state mood, self-harm, sex) were held out of the indicator set and related to class membership only after the measurement model was fixed, preserving a person-centered-then-validate design.

**Measures.**
- *Irritability* — 4 items from the IDA (Irritability, Depression, and Anxiety) interview, all drawn from its irritability subscale (not the depression or anxiety items), each scored 0–3 and summed (range 0–12); prorated if at least 3 of 4 items were scored, NA otherwise.\
- *Aggression* — CASI (Child and Adolescent Symptom Inventory), Category C, 8 items, each scored 0–3, averaged; required ≥ 6 of 8 items scored.\
- *Impulsivity* — CASI, Category A, items 16–18, each scored 0–3, averaged; required ≥ 2 of 3 items scored.\
- *Psychosocial functioning* — A-LIFE (Longitudinal Interval Follow-up Evaluation, adolescent version): aps205 (interpersonal functioning) and apr209 (global social adjustment). Raw admissible codes are 0–7 (aps205) or 1–7 (apr209); "No Information" is recoded to NA, leaving a scored range of 1–5 on each item, oriented higher = poorer functioning.\
- *State mood* — YMRS (Young Mania Rating Scale, 11 items, range 0–60) for state mania and CDRS-R (Children's Depression Rating Scale–Revised, 17 items, range 17–113) for state depression, both scored on the clinician summary rating; both already oriented higher = more severe. Full item lists and per-item valid-code ranges are documented in the companion scoring map.\
- *Self-harm* — YRBS-HS (Youth Risk Behavior Survey, high-school form) non-suicidal self-injury (NSSI), all 11 method-frequency items, summarized as a winsorized (capped at 8) sum, N = 310.\
- *Sex* — 1 = male, 2 = female, analyzed descriptively (chi-square on modal class), not entered into BCH.

**Analytic samples.** The complete-case main analytic sample is N = 435 (of 439 in the PGBI-elevated subsample that completed the SCID-II BPD questions). One measurement model, fit once on the full sample, is reused to assign each respondent's class from their own SCID responses, so "Class 1..K" refers to the same set of classes throughout. The **main analysis** pairs classes with concurrent validators (irritability, aggression, impulsivity, functioning, state mood) at each person's most recent completed SCID assessment (N = 435). A separate **self-harm analysis** (NSSI, N = 310) uses each person's own most recent assessment point at or before a common cutoff, chosen so that the self-harm questionnaire (which was not administered at the latest assessment waves) was actually given at that point; youth whose only completed personality assessment came after that cutoff are excluded from this analysis, which is why its N is smaller than the main analysis's.

**Enumeration.** K = 1 to 6 were fit with a full panel of fit indices (AIC, BIC, sample-size-adjusted BIC, CAIC, relative entropy, smallest-class proportion), cross-checked with the bootstrap likelihood-ratio test (BLRT) in `glca`, since `poLCA` provides neither BLRT nor LMR. Indices disagreed: BIC and CAIC bottomed at K = 2, aBIC and AIC bottomed at K = 5, and the nested BLRT was significant through the 5-vs-4 comparison. K = 5 was selected as the primary solution on a priori direction for this exploratory poster, a choice that also happens to align with the aBIC-favored model; K = 3 and K = 4 are reported as disclosed sensitivity solutions. One K = 5 class (n = 20, 4.6%) falls below a <5%-class exclusion convention; it was retained to provide insight into a clinically notable class that had a self-harm signal.

**Class-to-validator differences.** A manual bias-adjusted three-step (BCH) related classes to distal outcomes (Nylund-Gibson, Grimm & Masyn, 2019), avoiding the one-step, LTB, automated three-step, and classify-analyze approaches, all of which are biased by classification error or impose hidden restrictions. Each individual's classification-error matrix D was inverted (D⁻¹) and its rows used as third-step weights. Because base R's `glm`/`lm` reject the negative prior weights BCH produces by construction, class means, their full covariance, the omnibus Wald test, and pairwise contrasts were computed directly in closed form from the weight matrix rather than through a weighted regression call. Outcomes with more than 97% of values in a single category were routed to descriptive prevalence-by-class reporting instead of BCH. The closed-form estimator was independently validated against Mplus's native categorical-mixture BCH implementation (a different computational path — pseudo-class expansion with a cluster-robust sandwich) in three steps: recovery of known true class means in simulated data, replication of poLCA's log-likelihood and class sizes on the real K = 5 fit, and matching BCH-adjusted class means on a real validator.

**Missing data.** `poLCA` performs listwise deletion internally; the complete-case main-analysis subset (8 SCID-II BPD items non-missing) was built explicitly beforehand, with an ID column carried through so class assignments merge back by ID rather than row position. Each validator has its own completion rule (above): IDA requires ≥3/4 items, CASI Category C aggression ≥6/8, CASI Category A impulsivity ≥2/3, SCID completion is anchored at ≥6/8 items per wave.

**Framing.** Every validator is measured at the same time as (or immediately alongside) the personality assessment used to define the classes. This design supports showing that the classes differ on meaningful external measures, not that class membership predicts later outcomes; no forward-looking / predictive claims are made anywhere in this summary.

**Inference.** Design-based robust (linearization) standard errors were used throughout the BCH step. P-values were FDR-corrected (Benjamini-Hochberg) within each validator panel. Effect sizes are reported as ω² values for continuous validators. Unlike the BCH pairwise contrasts and p-values above, these effect sizes are computed on each person's *modal* (hard-assigned) class rather than the BCH weight matrix: ω² requires a standard sum-of-squares or contingency-table decomposition, and BCH's classification-error weights are negative by construction, which would leave that decomposition unbounded. Modal assignment does not correct for classification error, so it is subject to the same attenuation-toward-the-null bias that BCH exists to remove — these effect sizes should therefore be read as a conservative, relative indicator of which validators separate the classes most strongly (comparable across rows of the same panel), not as a precise standalone magnitude for any one validator, and should not be expected to numerically agree with the significance pattern in the BCH pairwise contrasts. All pairwise class contrasts are reported, not just the omnibus test, displayed as compact-letter displays (classes sharing a letter do not differ after FDR correction).

**Software.** R, using `poLCA` (Linzer & Lewis, 2011) for class enumeration and posterior class probabilities, `glca` for the bootstrap LRT, and base R for the closed-form BCH estimator described above.

**Limitations.** All reported validation is concurrent; a later analysis deferred to a manuscript will include a prospective panel. The smallest K = 5 class (n = 20) falls below this project's own class-size convention and should be interpreted with corresponding caution.
